# Blackboard lesson sources

One row per lesson: the question it answers, the rules and formulas it uses, where they come from, who wrote it, and the reviewer's sign-off. Required by the clean-room rules in `SHOP-ADVISORY.md` §6.

**How these lessons were made.** Claude (Anthropic's AI) drafted the lesson text, figure geometry, exercises and sample drawings fresh for WithShalom Studios: Shop on 2026-10-09, Drafting on 2026-10-12. Nothing was copied from other CAD or CAM tutorials, vendor manuals or other software's help. Because the text came from an AI tool, every numeric rule still has to be checked against the public source named here, and a working fabricator (Shop) or drafter (Drafting) has to sign off, before release.

**Every Shop threshold is a Shop setting.** Defaults are starting points. The shop overrides them in Shop settings, and each step says what its numbers depend on.

## Shop lessons

| 1 | Bend allowance | How long is the flat blank? | BA = θ(R + K·T); OSSB = tan(θ/2)(R + T); BD = 2·OSSB − BA; flat = A + B − BD. Starting K by R/T: <1 → 0.33, 1–3 → 0.40, >3 → 0.50. Hole edge to bend ≥ 2.5T + R. | Neutral-axis sheet-metal geometry, e.g. *Machinery's Handbook*, 31st ed. (Industrial Press, 2020), sheet-metal bending. K starting values: common shop practice; replace with measured K. | Claude, 2026-10-09 (adapted from the 0.5.1 lesson) | Pending |
| 2 | Bend deduction and the brake table | What number do I give the brake operator? | ISSB = tan(θ/2)·R; inside-dimensioned flat = a + b − 2·ISSB + BA; outside leg = inside leg + T·tan(θ/2); included angle = 180° − θ; n bends → n·BD; table at fixed R/T and K. | Same geometry as lesson 1. | Claude, 2026-10-09 | Pending |
| 3 | Minimum flange and die choice | Can this flange be formed? | V = 8·T default (6–12·T range); air-bent R ≈ fraction × V (cold-rolled steel 0.15, hot-rolled 0.12, 304 stainless 0.20, 5052-H32 aluminium 0.15); f_min = V/2 + OSSB − BA/2; largest die = 2(f − OSSB + BA/2). | The Fabricator, "How air forming works" (S. | 4 | Grain direction and bend radius | Which way should this part sit on the sheet? | Smallest R across and along grain as multiples of T, blended by cos²(angle to grain); 45° placement for two perpendicular bends. | Principle: general metallurgy of rolled sheet. **Numbers are conservative placeholders** (CRS 0.5/1.0, HRS 1.0/1.5, 304 0.5/1.0, 5052-H32 1.0/1.5 × T). | 5 | Holes, slots and reliefs | How close to a bend can a feature sit? | Hole ≥ 2.5T + R; slot ≥ 4T + R; relief ≥ T wide, ≥ R + T deep; smallest hole and web per process. | Common sheet-metal design rules of thumb (unchanged from 0.5.1). | 6 | Where bends meet | What happens where two bends come together? | Corner relief ≥ R + T past each bend line; jog height ≥ f_min; flange taller than channel width → punch-clearance warning. | Follows lessons 3 and 5. | 7 | Kerf and your dimensions | Is the hole I drew the hole I'll get? | Uncompensated path: hole = d + k, part = W − k. Compensation offsets the path k/2 toward the scrap. Inside corner R ≥ k/2. | 9 | Lead-ins and pierce points | Where does the cut start? | Pierce mark ≈ 2 (laser) or 2.5 (plasma) × kerf; lead-in = max(min, factor × T) (laser 0.5T, min 1 mm; plasma 1.0T, min 3 mm); hole for a clean start ≥ pierce + 2 × lead-in. | General thermal-cutting practice. **All numbers are placeholders**; measure from cut parts. | Claude, 2026-10-09 | Pending |
